# Hook Frameworks

Use these when generating, rewriting, or judging hooks.

For more variety, load `hook-families.md`. For on-screen text and covers, load `text-hook-templates.md`.

## Hook Jobs

Pick the job before writing the line:

- self-identification: make the viewer think, "this is about me"
- open loop: make the viewer need one unresolved answer
- stakes: make the information feel urgent now
- belief break: challenge a common assumption
- validation: name a fear or insecurity without blaming the viewer
- authority: show why the creator can speak on the topic
- demo bridge: make the viewer want to watch the thing happen

## The Three Rules

Every hook must pass these:

1. Never attack the viewer.
   Pull the viewer in through shared pain, desire, or situation. Do not call them dumb, lazy, broken, or wrong. Accusatory hooks trigger defensive scrolling. "Your funnel is broken" attacks. "Most funnels miss this one thing" pulls in.

2. Speak to desire, not homework.
   A task is not a hook. A capability, outcome, or felt relief can be.
   Weak: "You should post more content."
   Better: "How to get more clients from the content you already make."

3. Make the payoff visible.
   The viewer should know what staying gets them. The strongest container is number plus timeframe plus clear outcome, used only when truthful.
   Weak: "Fix your squat."
   Better: "Fix knee pain in 20 seconds."

## Two-Pronged Hooks

When a Reel has both spoken audio and on-screen text, they should do different jobs.

- Visual text: the concrete action, metric, claim, or scenario. The "what happened."
- Spoken line: the hidden mechanism, contradiction, context, or story behind it. The "why" or "how."

Splitting the angles makes the viewer process both tracks at once. Neither resolves alone, so scrolling costs them an answer.

Bad:

```text
Text: How to get better hooks
Spoken: Here is how to get better hooks
```

Better:

```text
Text: My hooks got 4x stronger when I stopped writing hooks first
Spoken: The line was never the hard part. The hard part was deciding what to hide.
```

Classic examples of the format:

| On-screen text | Spoken line |
|---|---|
| I quit my $200K job on Tuesday | My boss said one sentence that made it the easiest decision of my life. |
| I stopped texting first for 30 days | What I found out about the people in my life, I was not ready for. |
| I woke up at 4AM for 90 days straight | The results had nothing to do with mornings. |

When to use: any time the creator can make a bold, completed-action claim that is true.

When not to use: when no true claim exists. Do not fabricate a story or number for the visual just to fit the format. Use a template from `text-hook-templates.md` instead.

## YouTube-Title Text Hooks

Treat on-screen text like a thumbnail title. It should be short, obvious, and packed with niche language.

Good text hooks usually include:

- viewer keyword
- pain keyword
- outcome keyword
- timeframe, number, or named mechanism when available

Examples:

- "Fix low hook rate in 23 seconds"
- "The 3-day gym restart"
- "Stop losing viewers after line one"
- "The demo that beats a case study"

For the full keyword method and fill-in templates, load `text-hook-templates.md`.

## Two-Sentence Amplifier

Use two beats when one sentence is too thin. Beat 1 states the claim. Beat 2 amplifies it with one of four moves:

1. Magnitude bump. Beat 2 raises the scale or stakes.
   "Most creators trigger one type of dopamine. The viral ones trigger six."
2. Contrarian reframe. Beat 2 inverts the apparent point of beat 1.
   "I tested 6 AI app stacks. Five of them slowed me down."
3. Comparison. Beat 2 introduces a benchmark that makes beat 1 land harder.
   "We tested cold email for 90 days. The reply rate beat our paid ads."
4. Mechanism payoff. Beat 2 hands the viewer the reason to keep watching.
   "Your hook is not the hard part. The sentence after it is."

The filler test:

- Delete sentence 2. If the hook still works, sentence 2 was filler.
- Delete sentence 1. If the hook still works, sentence 1 was throat-clearing.

A sentence 2 that rephrases sentence 1 is not an amplifier. It must add new information, a new angle, or new tension.

## Identity Stack

Use this for educational, authority, or belief-shift Reels. The whole opening is about the viewer. No "I" until the stack is complete.

Structure:

1. Stack 4-6 lines about the viewer. Each line adds a new concrete detail of their exact situation.
2. Widen once with "or even" to catch the adjacent viewer one step ahead or behind.
3. Name the viewer's pain and enemy without blaming them. The enemy can be their own coping loop.
4. Name the specific desire, with a number or before/after when truthful.
5. Name the mechanism without explaining it. Give it a label and repeat the label. The repetition makes the unexplained thing feel like the key.
6. End with an unresolved promise.
7. Bring in the creator only after the viewer feels seen, and only as evidence.

Example:

```text
If you started posting this year,
or even if you have been posting for months,
and your follower count still barely moves,
and every advice video makes it sound like you are one tweak away,
but your actual problem is that nobody knows why they should stay,
you need to understand retention hooks.
Not hook lines. Retention hooks.
```

Craft notes:

- Trailing pauses ("...") force the slow drag that makes the accumulation land.
- Keep the language small and spoken. "You're sitting there stuck" beats anything polished.
- Name the coping behavior ("you keep telling yourself it's the next tool") instead of insulting the viewer. It mirrors without attacking.
- Do not pivot to "I" before the stack is complete.

For a fully annotated example and the identity-specific quality checks, load `command-identity.md`.

## Value Tease Ladder

The opening should give partial value and create the next question.

Pattern:

```text
hook gap -> partial answer -> deeper question -> example -> rehook -> mechanism -> payoff
```

Avoid:

```text
hook -> full answer -> generic tips -> weak CTA
```

## Rehooks

Use rehooks inside the first 3-10 seconds and at topic shifts.

Examples:

- "But here is the part people miss."
- "Now watch what changes."
- "You are probably thinking this only works if..."
- "The weird part is..."
- "This is where most people lose the viewer."
- scene switch
- object reveal
- crossed-out weak line
- blurred text reveal
- before/after comparison

## Caption As Second Hook

The caption should not repeat the exact spoken hook. It can:

- name the hidden mechanism
- tease the ending
- add proof context
- invite a comment around the unresolved question
- give a save reason when the post is genuinely useful
