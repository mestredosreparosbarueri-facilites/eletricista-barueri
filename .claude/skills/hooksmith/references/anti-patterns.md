# Anti-Patterns

Reject or rewrite these before final output.

## Fake Proof

Never invent:

- views
- followers
- revenue
- client results
- case studies
- timelines
- before/after outcomes
- "I tested this" claims
- "we analyzed X posts" claims

If proof is missing, use a no-proof hook family: belief break, validation, demo bridge, identity stack, or practical scenario.

## Viral Guarantees

Do not promise virality. Avoid lines like:

- "This will go viral"
- "Guaranteed viral hook"
- "Use this to blow up"
- "This hook always works"

Say what the hook is designed to do: make one specific viewer care, open a loop, and point to a real payoff.

## Viewer Attack

Do not blame or insult the viewer.

Weak:

```text
Your hooks suck because you are lazy.
```

Better:

```text
Most weak hooks fail before the topic even starts.
```

## Throat-Clearing

Cut warmup phrases:

- "Today I want to talk about"
- "Let me show you"
- "Here is the thing"
- "So basically"
- "What I want to share"

Start with the line that carries value.

Exception: "let me show you" can work in a demo when the visual begins immediately.

## Hedged Questions

Default to declarative hooks. Questions are allowed only when they carry a specific claim.

Weak:

```text
Have you ever wondered why your content is not growing?
```

Better:

```text
Most creators lose the viewer in the sentence after the hook.
```

## Stranger Pronouns

Do not open with "she", "he", or "they" about a person the viewer has not met.

Weak:

```text
She didn't call back, and that's when I knew.
```

The viewer has no stake in a stranger. Name the viewer, the situation, or the role instead:

```text
The lead didn't call back, and most owners never find out why.
```

## Equation Hooks

Do not open with X equals Y poster lines:

- "Consistency = growth"
- "Discipline = freedom"
- "Attention = currency"

They read as wall art. No viewer, no loop, nothing withheld. Turn the equation into a claim with stakes:

```text
Posting daily did nothing for me until I changed what the first line was doing.
```

## All-Caps Punch Stacks

Do not stack one-word punches in caps:

```text
STOP. POSTING. DAILY.
```

It reads as shouting and carries no information. Say the claim once, in plain case, with a real detail attached.

## Series Metadata First

Do not start the spoken hook with:

- "Day 4 of..."
- "Part 2 of..."
- "Episode 7..."

Put metadata in a small label, caption, or later context. The first beat must make this specific video worth watching.

## Vague Superlatives

Flag vague hype unless a concrete detail sits beside it:

- best
- ultimate
- game-changing
- powerful
- incredible
- amazing
- life-changing

Weak:

```text
The most powerful hook strategy ever.
```

Better:

```text
The 2-line hook structure that keeps viewers past the setup.
```

Detection rule: if a hook contains one of these words without a number, named thing, timeframe, or outcome in the same sentence, flag it and rewrite.

## Same-Angle Spoken And Visual

If the spoken hook and on-screen text repeat the same idea, rewrite one of them.

## Payoff Too Small

If the hook promises a big reveal but the body only gives obvious advice, reduce the promise or strengthen the payoff.

## No Real Viewer

If the hook could apply to anyone, it usually holds no one.

Add:

- niche
- scenario
- desire
- pain
- constraint
- proof
- format
